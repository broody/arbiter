# Trust model

Who has to trust whom, and for what. The short version: the chain trusts only
your rules and cryptography; players trust nobody for correctness; and players
trust infrastructure, where they use it, only for liveness. The exception is
the referee of a timed game, which players trust with time.

## The chain

The channel commits a state only if it's a replay of signed steps from the
committed anchor, directly or through a dispute candidate that was one, run
onchain (`submit_history`) or proved (the adapter). So the chain trusts:

- **your `GameRules`**, which must be deterministic and panic on illegal
  actions, and whose `max_steps` and `adjudicate` end any game that runs too
  long;
- **STARK-curve ECDSA and Poseidon**;
- **Starknet's proof verification**, for games settled with proofs;
- **the namespace owner's allowlist of adapter classes.** An allowlisted
  adapter can settle any game that names it. The counter's adapter has no
  admin, no upgrade path and no custody, and it pins one virtual OS program.

Signatures alone never commit a state. Two colluding seats can sign anything,
but they can't make the channel accept a state the rules wouldn't produce from
the anchor.

## Players

Each client verifies every signature and every step it receives, and never
signs from a state it hasn't verified. A player trusts the other player for
nothing:

| The other player… | Costs |
| --- | --- |
| sends an illegal or forged step | Nothing: it's rejected |
| submits an old state | Nothing, if you answer the dispute within the window |
| stops moving | Time: a dispute, then forced play onchain, then a win on timeout (`REASON_ABANDON`). In a timed game with a live referee, a flag once its time runs out |
| refuses to reveal randomness | Time: a stall ends in a forced reveal or a timeout |
| refuses to approve the result | Time: the result settles after one response window |
| equivocates | Nothing for you: two signatures by one key at one position are evidence |

A player's own risks are operational: losing the session key or the
randomness seed (a loss on time), and signing from a stale copy (see
[Signing safety](/concepts/signing-safety)).

## The keeper

A keeper archives, forwards and settles. It's trusted for **availability**, and
only by players who rely on it:

- **It can't forge a step.** It stores only steps that verify, and every client
  verifies again whatever it pulls.
- **It can delay or withhold steps.** Both players keep their own copies, so
  it's never the only one.
- **It holds no player keys.** Its account pays only for `submit_history`,
  `resolve`, the adapter's `settle`, and a referee's `acknowledge` and
  `resume_by_referee`, which anyone may send.
- **An offline player who relies on it to answer disputes** trusts it to be
  up. If it's down, or colluding with the opponent, a stale candidate settles
  after the window. Mitigations: your own client watching, more than one
  keeper, a longer response window.
- **Restoring on a new device from a keeper** also trusts it to serve the
  latest copy, unless your marks came with your key (see
  [Signing safety](/concepts/signing-safety)).
- Anyone can run one.

## The referee (timed games only)

The referee of a timed game is the one party players trust beyond liveness,
and only with time:

- **It can't** forge, reorder or edit moves, or settle anything the rules
  wouldn't produce.
- **It can** skew time or refuse to stamp. An honest seat's worst case is
  losing on time.
- **It decides where a dispute goes.** Its acknowledgement returns an
  unfinished dispute to offchain play instead of forced play, and its signature
  alone brings a game back from forced play. Neither changes a state: the
  channel still commits the best candidate, and a resume starts from the
  anchor.
- **It's accountable.** Two attestations at one seq with different transcripts
  are evidence of equivocation.
- **It's opt-in.** Its key is in the terms, and joining accepts it.

## The prover

A prover, hosted or self-hosted, sees public transcripts and signatures, never
keys. It can delay or refuse a proof but can't forge one: Starknet verifies the
proof, and the adapter checks that it proves exactly the expected transition.
`submit_history` is always there as a fallback.

## Privacy

Transcripts are public by design. The keeper serves them to anyone who asks,
`submit_history` puts the steps in calldata, and a prover sees every step it
proves. Referee suits games where every move is visible to both players. It has
no hidden-information support.
